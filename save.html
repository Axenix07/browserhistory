A brief history of Chrome

Google Chrome was first released in September 2008 for Microsoft Windows. It was built using free software components from Apple's WebKit and Mozilla's Firefox. Its minimal design and high-speed processing quickly made it a favorite among users.

By 2012, Google Chrome had surpassed Internet Explorer in global usage. Today, it is the most dominant browser in the world, serving as the foundation for many other browsers via the open-source Chromium project.

A brief history of Firefox

Firefox was created in 2002 by the Mozilla community under the code name "Phoenix." It was designed to provide a faster, more ergonomic, and more secure alternative to Internet Explorer. It was officially released in 2004 and became highly successful.

As an open-source project, Mozilla Firefox empowered users with extensions and themes, allowing for a level of customization that was revolutionary at the time. It remains a champion for privacy and open standards on the web today.

A brief history of IE

Internet Explorer (IE) was once the most widely used web browser, attaining a peak of about 95% usage share during 2002 and 2003. It was first released as part of the add-on package Plus! for Windows 95 in 1995.

Microsoft’s decision to bundle IE with the Microsoft operating system was a major factor in its dominance, though it also led to famous antitrust legal battles. Despite its early success, it eventually struggled to keep up with modern web standards and security.

Introduction

The World Wide Web began to enter mainstream use in the 1990s. While many people associate the internet with the web, the web is actually just one of many services that run over the internet. To access it, users require a web browser, a software application that retrieves and displays content from servers.

Over the decades, different browsers have risen and fallen in popularity, leading to what historians and tech enthusiasts often call the "Browser Wars." This competition has driven rapid innovation in web standards and performance.
